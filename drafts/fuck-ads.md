# Or, Why For-Profit Advertising on The Internet is Killing Democracy.

I, like almost everyone I know, worry about the state of Democracy. The vaunted ideals that were fought for in the Second World War are in such low supply, it really is hard to see the contemporary Western society as deeply alien, or to view that time and the ideals of the time as anything but alien from where we are.

Many things can be pointed at. Two World Wars within less than a century cannot have done anything less than to affect humanity at unthinkable levels of complexity ranging from macro to micro as these personal stories, connected together in larger and larger interlocking webs move through time.. Economic conditions change, and there is no doubt that a certain civil-mindedness that the Baby Boomers inherited and projected were helped substantially by post-war optimism in The West. That optimism has faded and been largely replaced by new, yet very abstract fears for the future all while we appear to be accellerating towards it (whether **it** is a cliff, loop-the-loop or endless highway of dreams), brick on the accelerator, our limbs pulled back from the controls by increasing G forces.

It's exhilerating to live in 'interesting times', but isn't it just so easy to look through a different lens at this embodied emotion as it shape-shifts between "exciting" and "way to fucking exciting for me, thanks WTF WTF! 😱"

I have a dear friend who has recently extricated themselves, incredibly painfully from an abusive relationship. They're out now, but it's tragicly clear to me that they are not yet *mentally* out of the relationship. She told me last week how she had begun to noticed, confront really, how many of her core beliefs about herself had shifted as she was told again, again and again how to think of herself as her then-partner imposed his beliefs about the world on her. Religion, or lack-thereof was never an issue at the start of their relationship, however over time, her loosly-Muslim boyfriend repeated his belief that "only through religion can one truly be moral". I believe I'm paraphrasing here but this was essentially the thing she was told, as an agnostic/atheist again and again. She told me there was no way that her her analytical mind accepted this argument, that a fear in God or Allah, is the only reason why anyone would choose to act morally, however over time and repetition, it didn't matter what she thought in a reasoned way. She truly began to question her own morality and that of her parents and anyone else without God.
